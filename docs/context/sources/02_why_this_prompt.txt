WHY THIS ROBUSTNESS PROMPT WAS DESIGNED THIS WAY
Credit-risk fairness project | 7 October 2026

The starting question

We already have two fitted credit-default models and a locked evaluation
split. This stage asks how sensitive their outputs are when the recorded
inputs are corrupted. We want evidence about reliability that is distinct
from predictive performance, fairness, explainability, and future monitoring.

The prompt therefore protects the frozen experiment first, specifies the
corruption design second, and asks for results only after both are verified.

1. What the attached article supports, and what the package adds

The attached article is:

risk measurement", Expert Systems With Applications 235 (2024), 121220.

2. Why SAFE tail swapping is the primary experiment

The project's evaluation is explicitly connected to SAFE. Running its genuine
RGR implementation gives the robustness stage a clear methodological anchor.
The inspected perturb function sorts a feature and swaps paired low/high tail
values. compute_rgr_values applies this individually or to a group of features
and calls rga with original predictions and perturbed predictions.

The model helper uses classifier predict_proba column 1. That makes class
ordering important: Codex must verify this corresponds to default=1. An
adapter may be needed for interface compatibility, but it must never fit or
change the model.

RGR is not simply Spearman correlation. The core computes an ordering-based
normalized concentration quantity with specific tie handling. Its values must
be interpreted from the actual implementation. Constant original predictions
can make the normalization undefined, which should be reported honestly.

3. Why p=0.05 is primary, with 0.01/0.025/0.10 sensitivities

The inspected package default is 0.05. Using that as the primary severity
reduces an arbitrary choice and keeps the main experiment close to the API's
default. The other three values are our prespecified sensitivity grid around
that default. They are not proven real-world error frequencies or universally
recommended SAFE severities.

The crucial distinction is between severity p and rows actually changed.
SAFE p=0.05 concerns both tails; it does not mean multiplying amounts by 1.05.
In the inspected source, the number of pairs is the smaller of
ceil(p*N) and N-ceil((1-p)*N). Twice that number gives nominal participants.
Ties can mean some participating rows retain the same value.

If N were 5,996, the nominal participants for our grid would be 118, 298, 598,
and 1,198. This is an illustration, not a verified current repository count.
The evaluation must compute actual counts, including changed values and the
union of changed rows in joint scenarios. Joint perturbation can affect more
rows than any single-feature swap because feature tails need not contain the
same customers.

We do not choose a severity because it gives an interesting model comparison.
The grid is fixed before outcomes are evaluated.

4. Why SAFE has an explicit holdout-input exception

Semantic noise scales should come from the training data. Authentic SAFE,
however, identifies tails in the evaluation inputs supplied to its function.
Replacing those with training quantiles would change the operation.

The allowed use of holdout inputs to define this prespecified transformation
is different from using holdout outcomes to tune a model, threshold, or noise
level. The prompt permits the former and forbids the latter. It also makes
the transformation data-dependent, which matters when defining uncertainty.

5. Why there is a second, semantic layer

Extreme-tail exchanges are a useful stress design but do not represent every
kind of input error. They can break relations between a customer's variables,
and numeric ordering of nominal category codes is arbitrary. Supplementary
tests make those limitations easier to assess without modifying SAFE itself.

Our supplementary choices are:
  - monetary fields: random signed changes of 10% or 20% of training IQR;
  - age: random signed changes of one or two years;
  - ordinary repayment delays: one or two valid ordered steps;
  - education/marriage: substitution with a different valid training category;
  - primary/stronger selected-record fractions: 10% and 20%.

IQR is resistant to extreme values and puts noise in understandable raw units.
A fraction of the individual amount would do nothing to zeros, particularly
relevant for payment fields. The prompt specifies fixed signed changes so
"10% IQR" cannot be reinterpreted as a Gaussian standard deviation, uniform
interval, or another unspecified distribution.

The selected-record fractions loosely align with SAFE's two-tail participation
at p=0.05 and p=0.10. This is an organizational choice, not equivalence of
stress intensity. SAFE exchanges extremes; semantic tests use smaller local
changes. Joint semantic scenarios concentrate errors in a common selected
subset and explicitly disclose that assumption.

6. Why PAY_* and monetary constraints need special care

Repayment status includes special states that may not form a simple equally
spaced sequence of months. Numeric adjacency alone is insufficient. The
updated prompt restricts ordered steps to confirmed ordinary delay levels and
requires disclosure when special states remain untouched.

Similarly, negative bill amounts can be legitimate credit balances; forcing
them to zero would invent a cleaning rule. Payments require nonnegative
constraints, credit limits need a documented valid lower bound, and age stays
integer. Clipping and boundary cases reduce actual changes, so we count them.
The prompt does not claim that marginal validity proves an entire synthetic
record is financially coherent.

7. Why we verify the baseline and preserve earlier artifacts

Any new result is only comparable to earlier stages if preprocessing, feature
order, row identity, class convention, frozen models, and split are unchanged.
Reproducing saved predictions is a practical check of the whole loading path.
Hashes help show that the robustness stage preserved existing evidence.

Threshold candidates explored earlier are not automatically approved operating
thresholds. The prompt requires actual prior threshold provenance, with 0.50
only a clearly labelled descriptive convention when necessary.

8. Why conventional metrics accompany RGR

Rank-oriented robustness can coexist with sizeable probability movement or
classification flips. A model can preserve ordering while moving scores
across an operating threshold. Conversely, scores may move without many
decisions changing. Movement, ranking, flips, discrimination, and probability
losses describe different consequences of the same corruption.

Observed outcomes remain fixed in these tests. Performance degradation therefore
measures sensitivity to corrupted records, not what would truly happen if a
customer changed payment behavior, age, or education.

9. Why identical inputs and paired comparisons matter

Both models must face the same customers, corruptions, and resamples. A paired
comparison removes avoidable variation from different random draws and allows
us to compare baseline-relative degradation fairly. It does not assume one
model must be more robust because it has better baseline AUC.

Thirty semantic repetitions and a fixed master seed are practical prespecified
choices for this task; they are not SAFE requirements. Monte Carlo spread
describes variation between synthetic draws. Sampling intervals describe a
different uncertainty, conditional on the declared procedure and sampling unit.
Repeated perturbations do not create additional independent customers.

Duplicate/predictor groups can require cluster resampling, while SEX summaries
require respect for subgroup composition. These are separate concerns. The
prompt prevents naive row resampling or forced stratification that splits
dependent clusters.

For SAFE, bootstrapping a fixed perturbed table answers a different question
from rerunning tail swaps in every resample. The prompt asks Codex to state
the estimand rather than attach intervals without explaining their meaning.

10. Why SEX results stay descriptive

SEX is unchanged and outside the predictive feature set. We ask whether the
same stress experiment produces different movement or degradation across
groups. Tail exposure can differ across groups, so response differences can
reflect who was changed as well as how the model responds.

We therefore subset the globally perturbed records for the main subgroup
summaries and count group exposure. We do not silently create a new within-
group tail experiment or call the results proof of discrimination.

11. Why drift/governance is excluded

This stage corrupts observations already in the evaluation set. Drift asks
whether the future population/environment differs from the reference period;
governance asks when that change should trigger review. Mixing them would
blur the research questions and increase implementation scope unnecessarily.

These notes define the intended experiment. They contain no executed
robustness findings, no verified current local model artifacts, and no claim
that the perturbation rates represent measured operational errors.
